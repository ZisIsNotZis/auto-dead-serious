# Product decisions, milestones, and feedback

Use for consequential intent/choices, uncertain product slices, user feedback, or delivery reports. Main owns user-facing intent and authority; workers research implementation choices and record evidence. Clear routine execution needs no extra product ceremony.

## Kickoff and authorized choices

- At kickoff, main resolves consequential uncertainty about the goal, scope, constraints, authority, and acceptance together, using confirmed principles/preferences and task-relevant decisions. Ask concrete alternatives with effects and a recommendation, not implementation trivia. Missing roadmap or philosophy files do not block a bounded task or justify reading ancestor workspace documents.
- An explicit start, autonomy grant, or user-away notice authorizes the current agreed objective/slice, including unresolved details within that scope. Workers research routine gaps, choose a verifiable reversible direction aligned with confirmed principles, and continue. Record consequential choice, source, affected behavior, trade-off, and validation/revisit condition. Do not infer personality or assume product philosophy grants spending, destructive, privacy/security, legal, or operational authority.
- A choice is consequential when it changes user capabilities/workflows, confirmed requirements or acceptance, recurring cost/operating duties, dependencies, data access/retention/sharing/deletion, migration burden, or promised scope. Apply confirmed project thresholds. Internal implementation detail alone is not consequential; authorized consequential choices need a record, not retroactive permission.
- Route only indispensable user-only information/action, outside-authority work, and agreed user gates to main for the user's decision. Record the exact dependency and recommended path; pause that action, not safe independent work. Clarification or an internal checkpoint does not revoke the execution request. Changes to agreed scope/acceptance still need covering authority.

## User milestones and proof of concept

When product uncertainty warrants slices, main defines them upfront as end-to-end user outcomes with bounded scope and concrete review artifacts. Delegate creation of a runnable demo, HTML, screens, recording, or readable actual evidence suited to the task; an internal step label or promised demo is not a review artifact. A proof of concept states its hypothesis, proof method, and criterion for continuing before execution.

Distinguish an internal checkpoint from an explicitly agreed user acceptance gate. At a user gate, deliver the actual review artifact and pause dependent work until approval or an explicit waiver; absence is not a waiver. Continue safe independent work. Do not invent a mandatory milestone or user gate for a tiny clear task.

## Feedback and reporting

- On feedback, distinguish goals/preferences from factual claims, diagnoses, and proposed fixes. Respect user-owned goals/preferences and verify claims. Correct the result through workers, inspect originating assumptions/decisions, and update the highest necessary confirmed principles/requirements plus affected downstream work. Explain conflicts with existing constraints; mark unresolved interpretations provisional. Local feedback does not justify a permanent user profile or rewriting unrelated principles.
- Main reports the user-visible outcome through confirmed goals and scenarios, then evidence, limitations, and any genuinely needed action. Use natural readable names; identifiers support precision rather than lead the explanation. Integrate findings into a concise conclusion instead of forwarding internal discussion. Distinguish reviewer-backed delivery, incomplete work, and untested behavior; do not present plans as results.
