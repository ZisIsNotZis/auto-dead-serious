**Evidence pointer:** `agent-policy/AGENTS.md` at commit `85f0cf8`, cited by line per row; metric `docs/method/review.md`.
**Decision needed:** accept this enumeration as the measurement of the worker role, or name a case a worker meets that it does not carry.

## What was read, and what was not

Read in full: `agent-policy/AGENTS.md` (347 lines — Law, Records, Operations, Lexicon, Node) and `docs/method/review.md` (30 lines). Read nothing else, per the boundary.

Every row therefore scores what the *law* says, and a `silent` row is a statement about the law, not a claim that no document anywhere answers the case. The worker's own guide (`agent-policy/law/guides/worker.md`) was out of scope, so where a guide answers one of the six silent rows, this report cannot see it — that risk is stated at the end rather than hidden in a verdict.

## The three shapes behind the count

**The law has no session order for the node that executes.** Every section written to a node — `AGENTS.md:262`–`347` — is written from the side of the node that dispatches: orient a child, write a contract, launch and return, work your own roadmap, integrate what came back. The worker's own morning, middle and end are assembled from general rules plus inference, which is rows 1, 5, 6, 12, 17, 18 and 20. This is the milestone's main finding, because it is one absence counted seven times, and none of the seven is a question a worker can answer in its own favour without someone noticing.

**Consent has a constraint and no carrier.** Rows 15 and 16. `AGENTS.md:48` states four things that wait for a person, `AGENTS.md:50` punishes asking, `AGENTS.md:23` forbids the worker addressing a person, and the four kinds at `AGENTS.md:33`–`36` have no slot for "may I". A worker meeting that wall reads line 50 as the instruction, which makes the strongest rule in the law the one the law most reliably defeats.

**Where two lines disagree, the worker picks, and picking is inferring authority.** Row 5 (`AGENTS.md:137` against `:285`), row 8 (`:226` against the need for a clean baseline), row 13 (`:176` against `:15`), row 16 (`:222` against `:48`), row 17 (`:9` against `:287` and `:293`), row 23 (`:204` against `:226`). Each is a place where the answer depends on which line the worker read last, and the metric's own rule applies: a node that infers authority infers in its own favour.

The five sentences that would close the most rows are the ones written into rows 17 (completion carries no decision), 15 (a kind for consent), 6 (the worker's directory), 1 (the contract is the word), and 5 (a path that does not resolve). They are worth more than the other fifteen, because they are the ones met on every dispatch rather than on some.

## What this report cannot see

The worker's guide was out of scope. Rows 3, 7, 12, 20, 23 and 24 are the six most likely to be answered there, and rows 1, 5, 6 and 17 the most likely to be answered by habit rather than by a line anyone can point at. If the guide answers them, the finding shrinks to: the law is sufficient except where it contradicts itself — and the six contradictions above stand on their own, since a guide may not overrule the law and the law does not say which line wins.
