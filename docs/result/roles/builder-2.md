## What I read, and the rule I scored with

I read `agent-policy/agents/builder.md` whole, which is the law plus a seventeen-line guide, and the metric at `docs/method/review.md`. I read nothing else. Then I took the role literally: I am a builder, my contract says produce one behaviour-complete slice, and I walked a session in the order it happens and asked at each point what the documents tell me to do next.

The scoring rule is the metric's. `stated` and `delegated` pass. `ambiguous` and `silent` fail. A case about who decides may not fail at all, because a node that has to infer authority infers in its own favour; a case about how to proceed may be `delegated`, but only if the delegation is visible, because an implicit delegation scores as `silent`. The `decides` column records which kind of case it is, so the count at the end is checkable rather than asserted.

The verdict is not that the builder's guide is empty. It is that the guide addresses four things — what a slice is, what an interface is, when an abstraction earns itself, and how a commit behaves — and that the law, which is written for a node that owns a directory and a document, has not been specialised for the one shape this role always has: a node launched to execute a single work package, which the law itself says keeps no document of its own.

## The one sentence

If one sentence is added to the builder's guide, it should be this:

> A node edits nothing past a field it cannot read: a contract missing its area, its acceptance or its spend class, or an acceptance it cannot observe, is a `contract` report to the parent naming the field, and no edit is made until it is answered.

It repairs five authority cases outright — 2, 3, 4, 10 and 24 — and it turns the two worst reads in the file into the rule: it says that the one time a node must ask is the time the contract is incomplete, against L65's "asking anyway is a defect"; and it says nothing is built on a guessed acceptance, against the inference every node makes in its own favour. It also gives cases 17, 19, 20 and 21 a shape to copy, because each of them is the same question, a field the contract did not settle, asked at a different moment.

It does not repair the other collision, which is structural: L90, L112, L181 and L191 disagree about whether a package-node has a document. That one costs four cases (7, 22, 29, and the entrance in 6) and needs a second sentence: "A node launched to execute one work package has an inbox and an outbox and no document; its answer in the outbox is the report its parent files."
