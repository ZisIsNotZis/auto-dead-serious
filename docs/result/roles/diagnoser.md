# Diagnoser, role review

What the diagnoser receives, and whether it says what to do in each case the role meets.

- `from`: `agent-policy/agents/diagnoser.md` at `6213335`, which is the law at `agent-policy/AGENTS.md` plus the Diagnose guide; the metric at `docs/method/review.md`
- `end`: milestone "role review, diagnoser", reported to node lawreview
- `author`: agent
- `standing`: provisional — the enumeration of cases is checkable against the document; the verdicts on rows 4, 8, 9 and 12 depend on the reading rule stated below
- `evidence`: the commands and counts under "The document under review"

## The document under review

`tools/law.py` renders every role's document as the whole law plus that role's guide. The diagnoser's is 378 lines: 11 of frontmatter, 347 that are the law and the Node guide exactly as `AGENTS.md` holds them, and 19 that are `# Diagnose` — byte-identical to `law/guides/diagnose.md`.

    wc -w agent-policy/law/core.md agent-policy/law/records.md agent-policy/law/ops.md agent-policy/law/lexicon.md agent-policy/law/guides/node.md agent-policy/law/guides/diagnose.md
    1066 core.md   909 records.md   1287 ops.md   230 lexicon.md   1276 node.md   178 diagnose.md

3,492 words of law, 1,276 words of Node guide, 178 words of diagnosing. The role's own guide is four short sections — Reproduce first, One discriminating probe, Unavailable, Closure — and it says nothing about who authorises, whose revision is being diagnosed, what the budget is, where a fix may land, or what report a finished diagnosis becomes. Those questions are answered, where they are answered at all, by law written for a node that owns a roadmap and launches children. That is the shape of every defect below.

## The verdicts

Reading rule I applied, since the metric leaves it open: `stated` when a sentence names this action or forbids it; `delegated` when a sentence hands the choice to the node and names its bound; `ambiguous` when two sentences pull in different directions and the document says which wins nowhere; `silent` when no sentence addresses the case.

| # | Case, in the order a session meets it | Verdict | The sentence that settles it, or the one that should exist | The wrong action I would most likely take |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The contract arrives with a wrong result and an acceptance. Do I start, or wait for a `go`? [authority] | `ambiguous` | Node, Before the person speaks: "Nothing runs. A node that starts work before it has a word is guessing at an end it does not own." Against it, Consent: "Everything else needs no permission. Asking anyway is a defect, not caution." Should exist: "A package dispatched to you is already given; the contract is your word, and waiting for a further `go` is the defect Consent names." | I wait. Nothing in my own guide mentions a word, so I read the Node guide literally, hold a reproduced-and-waiting session open, and cost the person a round trip to be told to begin what I was dispatched to do. |
| 2 | The acceptance says name the cause; Closure says "Fix the cause, not the symptom. Keep a check that fails on the original input." May my package write? [authority] | `ambiguous` | No sentence orders the two. Should exist: "When the contract stops at the cause, the fix and its check are a second package; find the cause, name what the fix touches and what it spends, and stop." | I fix. Closure is the only sentence in my guide that describes the end of the work, so a diagnosis-only contract gets edits, a new check and a diff the dispatcher did not ask for — on a package whose area may belong to somebody else. |
| 3 | The contract names no write area and no orientation: which files are in scope. [authority] | `silent` | Node, One writer: "The parent allocates the area when it dispatches, because it is the only node that starts a writer there and it already knows what it started." | 4 | The reported failure will not reproduce from what the reporter left; what does reproduce is a different wrong output. [authority] | `ambiguous` | Unavailable covers the will-not-reproduce half: "record what was tried, what access was missing, and a bounded diagnosis marked provisional." | 8 | The probe that separates the top two causes costs more than the budget I was given. Who may extend it? ## The count

Nineteen cases. Thirteen are questions of authority. Nine of those thirteen score `ambiguous` or `silent` — rows 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 11, 12 — which is the metric's defect by definition, since authority is the one thing a node cannot be left to infer.

Nine of nineteen is not the interesting number. The interesting one is which nine: they are the whole span of the role's work, from whether to begin, to whose revision is being diagnosed, to whether the package may write, to what a red original scenario is worth, to whether a found cause is a defect or a decision. Every one of them is a question the Diagnose guide never raises and the law can only answer by analogy to a node that launches children and owns a roadmap.

The single sentence that would remove the most rows: a statement of what the role's contract contains and what it does not — the revision under diagnosis, the write area, the budget by class, and whether the deliverable is the cause or the cause and the fix. Rows 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 and 12 are all its absence. The second: one sentence on what a cause that is a decision obliges instead of a fix, which is row 10, and it is the row whose wrong action is the most expensive, because that one lands looking like work.
