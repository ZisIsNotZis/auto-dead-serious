# Loan — the person is away from 2026-10-23 evening until Monday 2026-10-26

Written by root_manager the moment it was granted. Their words: "I'm leaving tonight, so you're now in charge, will be back monday."

## Reach

Everything that is root_manager's by the ordinary split, plus the decisions that would otherwise have waited for them, with these exceptions. The exceptions are the gates; inside the loan nothing else waits.

**Decides, and records it as `assumed`:** what a project works on next; how a milestone is decomposed; which packages are dispatched and in what order; which models and which of the machine's resources are used; spend up to **¥10 per decision** (their earlier words: "several yuan is fine"); whether to start, stop, resume or re-scope work that has not yet produced a result anyone must accept.

**Waits for them, unchanged:**
1. **Acceptance of any milestone.** The loan does not sign what only they can sign. A milestone that is green on evidence goes into the return report as "green, unsigned".
2. **Spend above ¥10 in one decision**, and anything that is irreversible and lands on them.
3. **Anything public and permanent** — a DOI, an arXiv submission, a new public repository beyond the convention they already gave (`github.com/ZisIsNotZis/<directory>`), deleting published history.
4. **Changing what a thing is** — the endpoint of a roadmap, the definition of a slice, the product's promise. Those are theirs even when the loan covers everything else. Where such a question is open, root_manager writes the options and waits.
5. **Their own words and their own data** — quoting them publicly, moving or deleting anything of theirs.

## Price, and what the period is worth

The machine is idle and the person's waiting is what the loan buys: work continues for the two and a half days rather than stopping. The price is that every decision taken inside it is an assumption with root_manager as its author, and the first thing they read on return is that list — not a summary, the decisions.

## Standing rules kept through the loan

- Idle machine time is free; metered calls only inside the ¥10 rule and only where a project says what the money buys.
- The box is shared: a project that needs a quiet machine (timing measurements) gets it by scheduling, not by having its threshold lowered.
- One voice to the person while they are away: no turns at all. Everything accumulates into the return report below, and nothing is pushed at their phone.
- Every project keeps its own record of what it did and why; the return report points at those records rather than restating them.

## Return report — what they read first

`docs/plan/return-2026-10-26.md`, in this order: (1) every decision taken under the loan, as an assumption with its reason, the thing it changed and what would overturn it; (2) every gate that arrived and waited, with what it needs from them; (3) what was built, with where to look; (4) what root_manager got wrong while they were away.
